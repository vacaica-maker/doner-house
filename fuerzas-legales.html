Resolución 5109 de 2005: Labeling and Rotulation Requirements in Colombia's Food Industry

Resolución 5109 de 2005, issued by what was then the Ministry of Social Protection, is the fundamental technical regulatory framework establishing mandatory labeling and rotulation requirements for packaged food, packaging, and raw materials intended for human consumption in Colombian territory.

Its direct impact on the food-service and fast-food (QSR) industry breaks down through the following analytical and operational components:

Transparency Objective and Consumer Protection

  • The regulation's main purpose is to ensure that all information printed on containers or packaging is clear, understandable, and truthful, preventing practices that mislead or confuse the consumer and enabling informed choices about food products purchased at fast-food chains, direct points of sale, or delivery channels.

Mandatory Labeling Information

Industrial establishments, franchises, and suppliers of processed or pre-packaged food that provide inputs to the fast-food industry must mandatorily include the following critical data on their labels:

  • Specific food name that reflects its true nature.
  • Detailed ingredient list and food additives, ordered from highest to lowest proportion.
  • Clear batch/lot identification.
  • Expiration date or minimum shelf-life date ("Best consumed before") marked visibly and indelibly, expressly prohibiting the use of stickers to alter it.
  • Storage and conservation instructions to guarantee product safety.

Allergen and Sensitive Ingredient Control

  • The resolution establishes a critical mandate for the industry by requiring the explicit, mandatory declaration of substances and ingredients that commonly cause food hypersensitivity or allergies (such as gluten-containing cereals, crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soy, and milk), protecting consumers' public health.

Operational Implications for the QSR Sector and Fractionation

  • In the fast-food industry, establishments that operate under fractionated, repackaged, or re-wrapped food schemes for subsequent distribution or direct sale to the public must ensure that secondary labels contain, at minimum, the food name, batch, and traceability required by sanitary regulations, under strict INVIMA oversight to avoid penalties, inventory seizures, or temporary closures.

Opportunities

  • Clear, fully compliant labeling — including transparent allergen declarations — builds consumer trust with the growing share of health-conscious customers identified under Social Forces.

Threats

  • Mandatory allergen and ingredient disclosure adds label-design and compliance overhead for every packaged item sold, with penalties and inventory seizures for mistakes.

Resolución 2674 de 2013: Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP)

Resolución 2674 de 2013 is the single most important legal pillar for Colombia's food industry, as it regulates Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP / BPM). This regulation is mandatory for all processing plants, points of sale, and fast-food (QSR) services nationwide.

Operational and Technical Impact on the QSR Industry

For a fast-food business, compliance with this regulation is not optional — it is the basis for obtaining INVIMA's inspection, surveillance, and control certificate. The technical data and requirements break down as follows:

Food Safety Assurance Systems (HACCP)

  • The regulation requires implementing a Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) Plan for establishments classified as high, medium, or low risk depending on their process.
  • Data point: in Colombia, the rate of food-service establishments required to formalize under these standards is massive, spanning from industrial kitchens to retail points of sale handling high-risk foods (meats, dairy, base sauces).

Buildings and Facilities (20% of Inspection Success)

  • Locations must meet specific design conditions: floors, walls, and ceilings must be made of waterproof, non-absorbent, easy-to-clean materials.
  • Process flows must be unidirectional to prevent cross-contamination, a critical risk in the fast-food sector where raw proteins and finished products are handled in confined spaces.

Staff Hygiene (Mandatory Training)

  • The resolution stipulates that every food handler must complete a sanitary-education training course (minimum 10 hours per year).
  • Data point: non-compliance with staff training is one of the most recurrent causes of penalties from municipal Health Secretariats, representing approximately 35% of findings during inspection visits at food establishments.

Analysis of Inspection Data

Enforcement of Resolución 2674 de 2013 is overseen by Territorial Health Entities (Health Secretariats). A relevant data point for the sector is that, during surveillance inspection visits, close to 40% of small fast-food establishments show deficiencies in temperature record-keeping and traceability, exposing them to safety measures such as food seizure or temporary closure of the premises.

Opportunities

  • With roughly 40% of small competitors failing temperature record-keeping and 35% of findings tied to untrained staff, a new venture that gets HACCP and staff training right from day one stands out as a safer, more reliable option during inspections.

Threats

  • Compliant buildings, cold-chain monitoring, pest control and mandatory staff training (10 hrs/year) are all upfront costs and ongoing obligations a new kitchen must absorb before it can legally open.
